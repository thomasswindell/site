Thomas Swindell

On Composition


Composition is a set of limitations placed onto a performer. These limitations can range anywhere from specific notes, strict form, dynamics, timbre etc. Composition can be something as vague as- I want you to play for 10 minutes. There is inherently a sense of hierarchy within composition. This hierarchical structure can be softened or blurred, although only by the composer which in turn means it cannot be fully abandoned. The act of instrument making is what I consider to be meta compositional. If you really want to go deep into this, nature is the ultimate composer but let’s skip that for today. In the example of viewing instrument maker as a composer, one is acknowledging the limitations existing within a given instrument. For example the wood or metal used. There is some power given to the player in this case such as type of strings, reed, how they use the given instrument to create sound, their own technical abilities etc. this proves that it is both possible and commonplace to perform multiple compositions at once, the composition of the physical properties of material, the composition of the instrument maker and players choices in materials used in creating and setting up the instrument, the composition of the players own physical technique, and finally the traditional composer, the improvisatory composition of the player or players, or a combination of both. In the same way you cannot escape limitations, you cannot completely eschew the hierarchy of music or art in general. Traditionally this hierarchy would follow this path: Nature/God/whatever you believe in, The artist/ the instrument maker, The composer( in a traditional sense of the word), The performer, The industry, The audience. This is not a definitive order although it makes the most sense historically. One can occupy multiple tiers, tiers can be skipped etc. for example. In fully improvised music, the composer tier is skipped. This may sound contradictory as I previously stated that compositional is unavoidable, however, the formal composer surely is. In much visual arts or non performing arts, the tier of performer is skipped and the tier of artist and composer are merged. In speech and vocal art and music, the tiers of nature and artist or instrument maker are merged. I will not list them all but there are many examples of variance from this hierarchy. The only constants are nature and audience. In an act of artistic decolonization, I will attempt to occupy the entirety of the intermediary tiers. Creating, composing, performing, and promoting( to whatever degree I feel necessary). This attempt should materialize in my E, EA, A, AA series.
